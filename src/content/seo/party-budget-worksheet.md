## Agree on the limit with your cohosts

Write down the total amount available for the gathering, who is contributing, and whether anyone expects repayment. A host covering dinner and a group splitting supplies need different arrangements. Have that conversation before someone places an order.

Choose a guest-count assumption and a date to confirm it. If your initial plan is for ten people, mark it as ten rather than quietly letting a few extra invitations change the cost. A per-person figure can help compare plans, but it does not replace checking fixed costs such as a room booking or equipment hire.

Decide which choices matter most. If the birthday meal is the priority, keep room for it before choosing flowers, favors, or extra activities. A smaller set of clear priorities makes later tradeoffs easier.

## Copy this party budget worksheet

Use one row for each purchase or booking. Add rows when different people are buying items in the same category. On a small screen, swipe the worksheet sideways to see all the columns.

| Item | Owner | Estimate | Committed total | Paid so far | Still due | Decision or payment date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food or restaurant | [Name] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Date] |
| Drinks and ice | [Name] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Date] |
| Cake or dessert | [Name] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Date] |
| Decorations | [Name] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Date] |
| Serving supplies or hire | [Name] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Date] |
| Other gathering costs | [Name] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Amount] | [Date] |

An **estimate** is a working allowance. A **committed total** is an agreed order or booking, including the deposit and remaining balance. **Paid so far** is part of that committed amount, not an additional cost. For example, a $100 order with a $25 deposit has $75 still due; it is not a $125 order.

Keep uncertain options separate until you choose one. Two possible cake orders should not both look like confirmed spending.

## Example: a $300 birthday dinner for ten

These are invented planning allowances for a dinner at home, not current prices or a promise that this amount will cover your gathering. Replace each allowance with your own quotes and shopping list.

| Category | Example allowance |
| --- | ---: |
| Food | $150 |
| Drinks and ice | $40 |
| Cake | $35 |
| Table decorations | $20 |
| Serving supplies | $15 |
| Unassigned reserve | $40 |
| **Total spending limit** | **$300** |

The planned purchases use $260, leaving $40 unassigned. Suppose the cake quote becomes $45. The cohosts can use $10 of the reserve, reduce another purchase, or agree on a different cake. Record the choice before ordering. Do not treat the reserve as a second budget on top of the $300 limit.

For the rest of the evening, pair this worksheet with the [birthday dinner checklist](/birthday-dinner-planning-checklist).

## Compare the idea and its whole cost

Before approving a purchase, check what comes with it. A decorated table might also need delivery, a vase, candles, or collection the next day. A restaurant plan may need a separate cake arrangement. Confirm any applicable fees and terms with the provider rather than guessing.

## Make the buying handoff explicit

Give each purchase one owner and a limit. A short message is enough:

> Jordan is ordering the cake by Thursday, up to [amount] including any confirmed extras. Please check with [host] before changing the order. We’ll settle shared purchases by [agreed method and date].

Check the worksheet after a booking, a guest-count change, or a major purchase. Keep receipts and payment arrangements wherever your group normally handles them; tracking event costs is separate from transferring money.

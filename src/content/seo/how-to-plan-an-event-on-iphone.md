## 1. Create the event home first

Before saving inspiration, create one place that holds the event’s name, approximate date, location, budget, people, and related moments. This can be a dedicated event planning app or a carefully designed system of native apps.

The test is simple: if another planner asks, “What is the current plan?” can you send one place instead of five screenshots?

In Gatherory, start with a workspace for the main event. Add the event name, date and time, optional location, budget cap, and cover. You can refine those details later.

## 2. Capture ideas without pretending they are decisions

The iPhone is excellent for discovery. You may find a venue in Maps, a visual reference in Photos, a restaurant in Safari, or a song in Music. Save the useful context—not just the URL.

For every idea, add:

- a clear title;
- the event it belongs to;
- a category;
- an estimated cost when relevant;
- one sentence explaining why it may fit; and
- the next question that must be answered.

This turns a saved link into something another person can evaluate.

## 3. Make decisions visible

Avoid deciding only through reactions buried in Messages. Use a shared decision state such as pass, consider, or approve. If a decision matters enough to affect the plan, its outcome should be visible without rereading the conversation.

In Gatherory, the Decide view presents open ideas one at a time. Loving an event idea approves it and can carry it into the Timeline, preserving the path from possibility to plan.

## 4. Use Apple’s built-in tools for what they do best

Your iPhone already has useful supporting tools:

- **Maps** for place research, directions, travel time, and shared locations;
- **Calendar** for personal availability and confirmed commitments;
- **Photos** for visual references and event memories;
- **Reminders** for personal tasks that do not need broader event context;
- **Wallet** for tickets, reservations, and travel passes; and
- **Messages** for conversation and quick coordination.

The mistake is asking each one to become the event’s master plan. Let a planning workspace connect the story, then use native apps as inputs and destinations.

## 5. Add collaborators deliberately

Invite the smallest useful planning circle first. Give each collaborator a real responsibility, such as venue research, music, transportation, guest coordination, or a related event.

Before sharing access, check:

1. Can they see the event they are helping with?
2. Can they add or change only what they should?
3. Are unfinished financial or private details appropriately limited?
4. Will their contribution remain attached to the correct event?

Gatherory separates hosts, collaborators, and guests so contributing to the plan does not automatically mean seeing or controlling everything.

## 6. Build the timeline from connected events

Add the moments that have their own logistics: walkthroughs, tastings, fittings, rehearsals, setup, the main gathering, and follow-up events. Each should have its own date, location, people, and planning status while remaining connected to the main event.

On a phone, this structure matters. A flat list becomes difficult to scan quickly; event context lets you understand why an item exists.

## 7. Prepare the invitation as a separate experience

Guests should not need to understand the planning workspace. Before sending an invitation, confirm that the guest-facing view includes:

- event name and purpose;
- date, time, and time zone;
- location and arrival guidance;
- dress or participation details;
- the schedule they are allowed to see;
- RSVP choices; and
- a clear way to contact the host.

Hide unfinished ideas, private notes, budget details, and planning-only events unless they are intentionally shared.

## 8. Run a five-minute mobile review

Before the event, open the plan on your iPhone and answer these questions without searching:

- What happens next?
- Where is it?
- Who owns it?
- What is still undecided?
- Who needs an update?

If any answer requires remembering which app or conversation contains it, move that context back into the event home.

## Keep the phone useful, not noisy

The advantage of planning on iPhone is that the plan is with you at the venue, in the car, and during the conversation where a decision happens. The risk is fragmentation. A calm event home lets every other iPhone tool stay helpful without becoming another place to check.

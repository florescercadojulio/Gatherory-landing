## Start with the people and the date

Friendsgiving can land on a work night, a travel weekend, or a day when people are balancing more than one celebration. Start by asking who is interested and what dates or time windows could work. A short list of realistic options is easier to answer than an open-ended “when is everyone free?”

Decide what kind of gathering you are hosting: a sit-down meal, an open-house window, or a casual potluck. The format affects the space, timing, and amount of coordination. Set a rough guest count before making plans that depend on capacity.

Choose a host or small organizing group. The host can make final calls about the home and timing, while cohosts can own the menu, contributions, or guest communication. Say how decisions will be made if preferences differ, especially for the date and format.

## Make a plan that fits the space

Walk through the gathering from the first arrival to the last person leaving. Consider where coats and bags will go, how guests will move between the kitchen and seating, and where food can be set out. If people are bringing dishes, identify counter or table space for them before everyone arrives.

Write down the practical details that guests will need: the address, arrival window, parking or entry notes, whether children or plus-ones are included, and what to bring. Keep private host notes separate from the message you send to everyone.

If the group is larger than the available seating, be clear about the format. A staggered arrival window or casual buffet may work better than implying there is a formal place setting for everyone. The plan should fit the actual home and guest list.

## Coordinate food and other contributions

Ask guests what they would like to bring, then fill gaps together. Avoid assigning a specific dish before checking whether someone has already offered it. Track the contribution, the person bringing it, and whether it needs serving utensils, fridge space, or a place to warm up.

Contributions do not have to be food. Someone can bring drinks, ice, cups, a playlist, a folding table, or help with setup. Let guests suggest what suits them, and make it easy to say they cannot bring anything.

Ask about dietary needs or access requirements in a considerate, direct way. Share relevant information with the people planning the meal, and tell guests what you can accommodate. Do not promise something the group has not confirmed.

### Example: a 12-guest potluck

For a group of twelve, one cohost can keep the main dish and serving space in view, while the other tracks guest contributions. Ask guests to claim broad categories first—such as sides, dessert, drinks, or a non-food task—then add specifics once you see what is missing. Keep a short list of what the hosts are providing, such as plates, utensils, and a serving area. The goal is a balanced table and a workable room, not twelve separate assignments.

## Set arrival, setup, and cleanup expectations

Choose an arrival window that gives the host time to finish setup. If a guest is helping in the kitchen, ask them to arrive earlier and agree on what they are doing. Everyone else can arrive when the gathering is ready for guests.

Create a simple host timeline: when to clear the table, set out drinks, receive contributions, serve the meal, and begin packing leftovers. Assign cleanup roles before the evening gets busy. A few specific offers—collecting dishes, wiping the table, or taking recycling out—are easier to act on than “we’ll clean later.”

Leave some open time in the schedule. Guests may arrive at different times, conversations run long, and a shared meal does not need to follow a minute-by-minute program.

## Send one clear guest note

Here is a message you can copy and adapt:

> We’re getting together for Friendsgiving on [date] at [time or arrival window] at [address]. We’re planning a casual [meal/open-house] for about 12 people. Please reply with what you’d like to bring, or tell us if another kind of help works better. We’ll coordinate the menu so we don’t double up. The easiest entrance is [note], and [parking/access detail]. Looking forward to seeing everyone!

Update the note when the date, address, or format is settled. If something is still open, say so and share when you expect to decide.
